## PART A — PROGRAM FOUNDATION

### 1. Purpose of this manual

This manual is the single operating reference for National Shield. It defines what the program teaches, how sessions run, what coaches must be certified in, how children are kept safe, and how the program is evaluated. Everything a coach needs to deliver a compliant, high-quality class is in here.

It exists for four reasons:

1. **Consistency** — every class delivers the same standard regardless of who coaches it.
2. **Safety and liability** — documented standards are what insurers, facility landlords, grant funders and parents expect from a registered non-profit working with minors.
3. **Credibility** — NAA is a new organization. A written program standard is the fastest way to earn parent trust.
4. **Scalability** — a documented curriculum can be handed to a second coach, a second venue, or a second city without diluting quality.

---

### 2. Positioning statement

National Shield is **not** a fight club, and should never be marketed as "MMA for kids."

> National Shield Martial Arts is a **fitness-first, safety-focused personal development program delivered through martial arts.** Participants become fitter, stronger, more confident and more aware of their personal safety.

The core promise to a parent:

**We do not teach children to look for fights. We teach them to avoid them, respond confidently, stay safe, and get help.**

Program philosophy, repeated in every single class:

> **Avoid the fight. Protect yourself. Get to safety. Get help.**

|---|---|---|

### 4. Alignment with Canadian sport standards

This is the section that separates National Shield from a commercial dojo. The program is deliberately built on recognised national and provincial frameworks.

| Framework | How National Shield applies it |
|---|---|
| **Sport for Life — Long-Term Development in Sport and Physical Activity (LTD 3.0)** | Active Start, FUNdamentals and Learn to Train develop physical literacy before puberty so children have the basic skills to be active for life. Age bands and training ratios below are mapped directly to these stages. |
| **Physical literacy** | The FUNdamentals stage balances structure with enjoyment, emphasising participation and physical literacy while introducing basic sport skills — this is why the 4–8 program is 60% movement/coordination, not technique. |
| **Sask Sport — Respect in Sport (Activity Leader)** | The program educates youth leaders, coaches, officials and participants aged 14 and up to recognise, understand and respond to bullying, abuse, harassment and discrimination. Mandatory for every National Shield coach. Certifications are valid for 5 years and must be renewed on expiry. |
| **Responsible Coaching Movement (CAC / CCES)** | Three components adopted in full: Rule of Two, background screening, ethics training. |
| **Rule of Two (Coaching Association of Canada)** | A coach should have another coach or screened adult present when interacting with participants, and the training environment should be open, observable and justifiable. |
| **UCCMS / Safe Sport** | Code of conduct, complaint pathway and discipline procedure adopted at organisational level. |
| **Canadian Guideline on Concussion in Sport** | Recognise → Remove → Refer → Rest → Return protocol, medical clearance required before return. |
| **NCCP (National Coaching Certification Program)** | Coaches who have completed the NCCP's Safe Sport Training receive equivalency recognition for Respect in Sport. Coaches are encouraged toward NCCP Fundamental Movement Skills / Coaching Children modules. |

**Note on Respect in Sport:** Respect in Sport is a required program for coaches registered with a Provincial Sport Governing Body in Saskatchewan, and the online training is provided free of charge. Because National Shield may operate outside a PSGB's direct membership, **NAA adopts it as an internal, non-negotiable standard regardless of whether an external body requires it.** This is a deliberate decision and should be stated publicly — it is a competitive advantage.

## PART B — PROGRAM STRUCTURE

### 5. Program streams

⚠️ **Key change from the draft concept:** the 9–17 band has been split. A 9-year-old in *Learn to Train* and a 16-year-old in *Train to Train* have different bone maturity, attention span, load tolerance and social needs. Coaching them in one group is the single most common quality failure in community martial arts programs, and insurers and parents both notice it.

| Stream | Ages | LTD stage | Class cap | Coach ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🟢 **National Shield Kids** | 4–8 | Active Start → FUNdamentals | 12 | 1:6 (min. 2 adults) |
| 🔵 **National Shield Youth** | 9–12 | Learn to Train | 16 | 1:8 (min. 2 adults) |
| 🟠 **National Shield Teen** | 13–17 | Train to Train | 16 | 1:10 (min. 2 adults) |
| 🔴 **National Shield CombatFit** | 18+ | Active for Life | 20 | 1:12 |

If registration in a band is too small to run separately at launch, run **9–17 combined** but split the room into two coaching pods for the SKILL and PROTECT blocks. Never coach a 9-year-old and a 16-year-old as one partner pair.

---

### 6. Training balance by stream

Percentages are of a 60-minute session and are the delivery standard, not a suggestion.

#### 🟢 KIDS (4–8) — *Move • Play • Learn • Grow*

| Component | % |
|---|---|
| Fitness, movement & physical literacy | 40% |
| Coordination & agility | 20% |
| Martial arts fundamentals | 15% |
| Personal safety | 15% |
| Confidence & anti-bullying | 10% |

**Develops:** running, jumping, balance, coordination, agility, reaction, mobility, basic bodyweight strength, focus, listening, following instruction, teamwork.

**Martial arts introduction:** ready position, basic stance, footwork, controlled hand techniques, basic kicks, pad work on coach-held mitts, blocking concepts, movement and reaction drills.

**Personal safety — kept to three words:**

> **STOP → MOVE AWAY → GET HELP**

Taught through: personal space, saying "STOP" with a strong voice, identifying trusted adults, leaving uncomfortable situations, telling an adult what happened.

**Absolute:** no sparring, no fighting language, no contact between children.

---

#### 🔵 YOUTH (9–12) — *Fitness • Skill • Confidence • Self-Defence*

| Component | % |
|---|---|
| Fitness & athletic conditioning | 40% |
| Martial arts skills | 25% |
| Self-defence & personal safety | 15% |
| Anti-bullying & confidence | 10% |
| Mindset, discipline & teamwork | 10% |

**Fitness:** cardio, agility, speed, reaction, balance, coordination, core, bodyweight strength, mobility, endurance, partner fitness, circuits.

**Martial arts sampling** — fundamentals from several disciplines, taught as movement skills, not as competitive styles:

🥊 Boxing · 🥋 Martial arts basics · 🦵 Kickboxing · 🤼 Wrestling movement · 🥋 Grappling fundamentals

This multi-discipline sampling is deliberate and defensible: the Learn to Train stage focuses on acquiring a wide range of skills rather than early specialisation in one sport.

---

#### 🟠 TEEN (13–17) — *Train • Compete With Yourself • Lead*

| Component | % |
|---|---|
| Fitness & strength development | 40% |
| Martial arts skill & technical depth | 30% |
| Self-defence & scenario work | 15% |
| Leadership, mindset & discipline | 15% |

Additions at this stage: structured strength progression (bodyweight → banded → light external load with technique competency), longer technical sequences, controlled partner drilling, situational self-defence scenarios, and a **Junior Leader pathway** (assisting in Kids classes under supervision — an excellent retention and volunteer-pipeline tool).

---

#### 🔴 COMBATFIT (18+) — *Train Hard • Move Better • Feel Stronger*

| Block | Time |
|---|---|
| Warm-up & mobility | 10 min |
| Cardio & agility | 15 min |
| Strength & conditioning | 15 min |
| Boxing / kickboxing / pad work | 15 min |
| Mobility & cooldown | 5 min |

Adults train hard without any requirement to compete or spar. Periodic dedicated self-defence modules: awareness, verbal de-escalation, boundary setting, escape principles, personal safety planning, emergency response.

> **The goal is to get home safely — not to win a fight.**

## PART C — CURRICULUM

### 7. The SHIELD Safety System™

This is the program's intellectual signature. Teach it as a system, not as "self-defence."

| Letter | Principle | Taught as |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | **See** | Awareness of surroundings, exits, and who is around you |
| **H** | **Have boundaries** | Knowing what behaviour is and isn't acceptable |
| **I** | **Intentionally use your voice** | Confident, assertive, loud when needed |
| **E** | **Exit** | Move away from unsafe situations |
| **L** | **Locate help** | Get to a trusted adult or safe location |
| **D** | **Describe & report** | Tell someone clearly what happened |

> **Self-defence starts before the fight.**

Physical escape technique is taught only after the first five letters are demonstrably understood. This ordering is intentional and should be explained to parents.

---

### 8. The BRAVE Anti-Bullying Program

A formal curriculum component with allocated class time — not a marketing line.

| Letter | Principle |
|---|---|
| **B** | **Be aware** — recognise bullying and unsafe behaviour |
| **R** | **Respond confidently** — body language and strong voice |
| **A** | **Avoid escalation** — don't turn a situation into a fight |
| **V** | **Voice it** — tell a trusted adult |
| **E** | **Exit safely** — move away and get somewhere safe |

**Core message:** *Asking for help is strength, not weakness.*

BRAVE is delivered through scenario role-play, not lecture. Coaches must be trained to run scenarios without re-traumatising a child who is currently being bullied, and must know the disclosure pathway (Section 16).

---

### 9. The 60-minute session architecture

Identical structure every week. Predictability is what makes children with focus difficulties succeed, and it lets parents understand exactly what they're paying for.

| Time | Block | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **0–10** | ⚡ **ACTIVATE** | Running, dynamic warm-up, reaction games, mobility, agility |
| **10–25** | 💪 **POWER** | Fitness circuit — squats, lunges, push patterns, planks, bear crawls, mountain climbers, shuttle runs, agility ladder, med-ball, partner challenges (age-scaled) |
| **25–40** | 🥋 **SKILL** | Stance, footwork, movement, pad work, controlled punches and kicks, defensive movement, reaction drills, partner coordination |
| **40–50** | 🛡️ **PROTECT** | Personal space, assertive communication, awareness, boundaries, escaping simple grabs, creating distance, moving to safety, getting help, anti-bullying scenarios |
| **50–57** | 🔥 **SHIELD CHALLENGE** | Reaction, agility, pad, relay, circuit or balance challenge |
| **57–60** | 🧠 **RESET** | Breathing, stretch, one-minute lesson, team message, team finish |

**Coach requirement:** a written session plan for every class, kept on file for 12 months. Template in Appendix B.

### 11. The 12-Week Foundation Cycle

Launch in defined 12-week cycles rather than an open-ended class. Cycles create urgency, a natural renewal point, a clean assessment moment, and a far better marketing story.

#### WEEKS 1–4 — BUILD

| Week | Theme | Content | Learning outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Welcome to National Shield | Baseline fitness screen, movement, basic stance, team rules, respect | Participant can demonstrate ready position and state the club philosophy |
| 2 | Balance & Movement | Footwork, agility, balance, reaction | Participant can move in four directions maintaining stance |
| 3 | Speed & Reaction | Reaction games, speed drills, pad work | Participant reacts to a visual cue with a correct controlled technique |
| 4 | Strong Body | Strength, core, conditioning, basic combinations | Participant completes the age-scaled circuit with correct form |

#### WEEKS 5–8 — PROTECT

| Week | Theme | Content | Learning outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Confidence | Body language, voice, boundaries | Participant demonstrates assertive posture and a strong "STOP" |
| 6 | Personal Space | Awareness, distance, safe positioning | Participant identifies exits and maintains safe distance |
| 7 | Anti-Bullying | BRAVE program, scenario discussion, assertive communication | Participant can explain all five BRAVE steps |
| 8 | Escape & Safety | Age-appropriate escape concepts, creating distance, moving to safety, getting help | Participant escapes a light wrist hold and moves to a safe adult |

#### WEEKS 9–12 — PERFORM

| Week | Theme | Content | Learning outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | Focus | Reaction, concentration, following instruction | Participant completes a 3-step instruction sequence unaided |
| 10 | Discipline | Fitness challenge, combinations, teamwork | Participant performs a 3-technique combination on pads |
| 11 | Shield Challenge | Agility, strength, coordination, pad work, fitness | Participant completes the full Shield Challenge circuit |
| 12 | **POWER • PROTECT • PERFORM DAY** | Parent demonstration + achievement recognition | Level assessment completed and awarded |

**Week 12 is a marketing event, not just a class.** Parents attend, watch a demonstration of fitness, agility, pad work, martial arts fundamentals, safety concepts and teamwork, and see their child receive recognition. Photograph it (with consent — Section 17), and it fills the next cycle.

### 12. Progression & recognition system

**Do not use traditional coloured belts** unless and until NAA formally affiliates with a martial arts governing body whose curriculum and grading standards it intends to follow. Awarding belts without that affiliation is a credibility risk with informed parents and other clubs.

Use the National Shield achievement system instead.

| Level | Name | Motto | Assessed on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🟢 **1** | FOUNDATION | *I Can Move* | Fitness baseline, basic movement, ready position, class etiquette |
| 🔵 **2** | POWER | *I Can Get Stronger* | Circuit competency, coordination, basic combinations |
| 🟠 **3** | CONFIDENCE | *I Can Stand Strong* | Voice, posture, communication, self-control under pressure |
| 🔴 **4** | PROTECT | *I Know How to Stay Safe* | Full SHIELD system, BRAVE steps, escape concepts, decision-making |
| ⚫ **5** | LEADER | *I Lead by Example* | Discipline, teamwork, responsibility, peer support, attendance |

**Assessment standard:** each level requires **(a)** demonstrated technical/physical criteria, **(b)** demonstrated understanding of the safety content, and **(c)** attendance of at least 75% of the cycle. Assessment is criterion-referenced against the participant's own prior performance — never ranked against other children.

**Recognition items:** certificate · wristband · achievement patch · National Shield T-shirt at Level 3 · public recognition at Challenge Day.

---

### 13. Monthly Shield Challenge Day

One Saturday per month. Four assessment domains:

- **FITNESS** — endurance, strength, agility
- **SKILL** — pad accuracy, footwork, reaction
- **MINDSET** — focus, discipline, teamwork
- **PERSONAL DEVELOPMENT** — effort, attitude, peer support

**Awards:** 🏅 Most Improved · 🏅 Best Team Player · 🏅 Greatest Effort · 🏅 Confidence Award · 🏅 Leadership Award

**Deliberately not** "fastest" or "strongest." Effort- and character-based recognition is consistent with NAA's non-profit youth-development mandate, keeps less athletic children engaged, and is what retains members.

Record each participant's Challenge Day results — a 12-month personal progress chart is the single most persuasive thing you can hand a parent at renewal.

## PART D — SAFETY, CONTACT & SAFEGUARDING

### 14. Contact and sparring policy

**This is the most important policy in the manual.** It must be published on the website, included in the registration package, and signed by every parent.

| Age band | Permitted | Prohibited |
|---|---|---|
| **4–8** | Focus mitts, kick shields, movement drills, games, non-contact partner activities | All sparring. All child-to-child striking. All head contact. Throws and takedowns. |
| **9–12** | Coach-held pad work, cooperative partner drills, light controlled wrist/grip escapes, ground movement | Sparring. Head contact of any kind. Competitive takedowns. Chokes and joint locks. |
| **13–17** | Progressive controlled partner work, light technical sparring **only** with full protective equipment, written parental consent, direct coach supervision, and demonstrated Level 3+ | Head contact for participants under 14. Unsupervised sparring. Chokes and joint locks below Level 4. Any "hard" or competitive sparring within the Foundation program. |
| **18+** | Controlled technical sparring by consent with full equipment | Unsupervised sparring. Sparring without a waiver on file. |

Any permitted contact must be **age appropriate, controlled, supervised, correctly equipped,** and consistent with this written policy.

> **Competition comes later. The Foundation program is fitness and skill.**

**Protective equipment for permitted 13+ sparring:** mouthguard, headgear, 14–16 oz gloves, shin guards, groin protection. Participant-owned, inspected before use.

### 15. Coaching standard

**NATIONAL SHIELD COACH STANDARD** — non-negotiable for every person coaching a youth class.

| Requirement | Detail | Renewal |
|---|---|---|
| Criminal Record Check + **Vulnerable Sector Check** | All personnel 18+ with access to minors, including board members and regular volunteers | Every 3 years |
| **Respect in Sport — Activity Leader** | Required for coaches and team personnel; valid 5 years, recertification required on expiry | 5 years |
| **NCCP Safe Sport Training** | Free CAC e-module | Once, refresh recommended |
| **Standard First Aid + CPR-C (AED)** | At least one certified holder present at every session | 3 years |
| **Concussion recognition training** | CAC / Parachute module | 2 years |
| Signed Code of Conduct & Ethics | UCCMS-aligned | Annually |
| NAA / National Shield orientation | Program philosophy, curriculum, contact policy, emergency procedures | On hire + annually |
| Two coach-supervised shadow sessions | Before independent delivery | On hire |

**Rule of Two — mandatory.** A coach must have another coach or screened adult present when interacting with participants, the environment must be open and observable, and coach/volunteer gender should be considered relative to the participants. Practically, for National Shield:

- Never one adult alone with one child. Ever.
- Change room and washroom supervision: no adult enters alone with a child; parents handle 4–8 washroom needs.
- **No one-to-one electronic messaging with a minor.** All communication goes to a group channel or copies the parent.
- Doors to the training space remain observable; parents may watch (see viewing policy, Section 18).

### 16. Safeguarding & disclosure pathway

Coaches will, sooner or later, receive a disclosure — the anti-bullying curriculum makes it more likely, not less. Every coach must know this pathway before their first class.

1. **Listen. Do not investigate.** Do not ask leading questions or press for detail.
2. **Do not promise confidentiality.**
3. **Record** what was said, in the child's words, in the Incident/Disclosure log the same day.
4. **Report immediately** to the NAA Safeguarding Lead (a named director; not the coach's direct supervisor if that creates a conflict).
5. Where a child is believed to be in need of protection, **the duty to report to the Ministry of Social Services / police is a legal duty and is not discretionary.** It is not deferred pending internal review.
6. Maltreatment within the sport environment is additionally handled under the NAA complaint and discipline policy.

Appoint and publish a named **Safeguarding Lead** and an alternate before the first class runs.

### 17. Risk management

**Emergency Action Plan (EAP)** — a written, venue-specific EAP posted at every training location and reviewed with all coaches:

- Location of the nearest phone, AED and first aid kit
- Exact venue address and nearest hospital (read out loud in an emergency — people forget addresses)
- Designated charge person and call person for each session
- Access route for emergency services, and who unlocks/directs
- Emergency contact retrieval process

**Concussion protocol — Recognise · Remove · Refer · Rest · Return.** Any suspected concussion means immediate removal from the session, parent notification, and **written medical clearance before return.** No exceptions and no coach discretion. Adopt a graduated return-to-activity progression.

**Pre-participation screening:**
- All participants: medical/allergy/medication disclosure form, emergency contacts, photo consent
- Adults: PAR-Q+ completed before first session
- Asthma inhalers and epinephrine auto-injectors stored accessibly, coaches trained in use, action plan on file

**Facility & equipment standards:**
- Matted training surface, inspected for gaps and tears before every session
- Minimum clear training area per participant; hard edges, mirrors and columns padded or coned off
- Pads, mitts and shields sanitised after every class and inspected weekly; damaged equipment removed from service immediately
- Hand hygiene before and after partner work; skin-infection exclusion rule published (open wounds covered; suspected ringworm/impetigo excluded until cleared)
- Water breaks scheduled in the POWER and SKILL blocks, not left to request

**Insurance:** NAA must hold its own Commercial General Liability coverage plus participant accident coverage, with the facility named as additional insured where the lease requires. Confirm in writing that the policy covers **martial arts instruction and controlled contact activity** — many general sport policies exclude combat sport, and this is a common and expensive discovery made after an incident.

**Documentation retained on file:** waivers and assumption-of-risk agreements, registration and medical forms, attendance registers, session plans, incident reports, equipment inspection log, coach certification register.

---

### 18. Parent & communication policy

- **Registration package** includes: program manual summary, contact/sparring policy, code of conduct, refund policy, photo consent, medical form, and the parent handbook.
- **Viewing:** parents are welcome to observe. An open-door policy is both a safeguarding control and a sales tool. Designate a viewing area outside the training space so classes are observable but not disrupted.
- **Communication:** group email or a group messaging channel only. No coach-to-minor direct messaging. All coach social media accounts remain separate from personal accounts.
- **Photo/video:** written consent per participant, recorded and honoured. No child's full name published alongside their image. Consent is revocable at any time.
- **Privacy:** personal information collected only for program administration, stored securely, retained per policy, and not shared with third parties without consent.
- **Behaviour:** a published participant and parent code of conduct with a clear escalation path (verbal reminder → parent meeting → temporary suspension → withdrawal).

## PART E — OPERATIONS

### 19. Membership & fee framework

Structure, not final pricing — validate rates against Regina market comparables (community soccer, gymnastics, karate and dance clubs) and against NAA's cost per session before publishing.

| Option | Description |
|---|---|
| **Trial class** | One free or low-cost class, no commitment. Highest-converting acquisition tool you have. |
| **1-Day membership** | Friday **or** Saturday, per 12-week cycle |
| **2-Day membership** | Friday **and** Saturday, per 12-week cycle (discounted vs. 2× single) |
| **Family rate** | Second and subsequent siblings discounted |
| **Adult CombatFit** | Per cycle or 10-class pass |
| **Drop-in** | Adults only |

**Non-profit obligations and opportunities:**
- **Publish a financial-assistance pathway.** As a registered non-profit, NAA should have a subsidy or bursary route and should signpost KidSport Saskatchewan and Canadian Tire Jumpstart on the registration page. This is both mission-aligned and a strong differentiator against commercial dojos.
- Fee structure must be defensible against NAA's non-profit purpose — surplus reinvested in programming, equipment and subsidy.
- Registration fee should be itemised: instruction, equipment use, insurance/administration, achievement materials.

**Refund policy** — publish before launch: pro-rated refund on medical withdrawal with documentation, defined cut-off for voluntary withdrawal, credit rather than refund for missed individual classes, and the club's cancellation/make-up procedure.

## APPENDIX A — Pre-Launch Compliance Checklist

Nothing runs until every box is ticked.

**Governance & legal**
- [ ] Program approved by NAA Board and minuted
- [ ] CGL insurance in force, combat/martial arts activity confirmed in writing
- [ ] Participant accident insurance in force
- [ ] Facility lease/licence signed; additional-insured requirements met
- [ ] Waiver, assumption of risk and registration forms legally reviewed

**Policies published**
- [ ] Contact & sparring policy
- [ ] Code of conduct (participant, parent, coach)
- [ ] Complaint & discipline procedure
- [ ] Screening policy
- [ ] Concussion policy
- [ ] Photo/media consent & privacy policy
- [ ] Refund & withdrawal policy
- [ ] Financial assistance pathway

**People**
- [ ] Safeguarding Lead and alternate named and published
- [ ] All coaches: VSC clear, Respect in Sport, Safe Sport, First Aid/CPR-C, concussion module
- [ ] Coach certification register created
- [ ] Orientation and shadow sessions completed

**Operations**
- [ ] Venue-specific EAP written and posted
- [ ] First aid kit stocked, AED location confirmed
- [ ] Phase 1 equipment purchased and inspected
- [ ] 12-week session plans written for each stream
- [ ] Registration system live
- [ ] Attendance and incident logs in place
