We used to get blank stares when we mentioned a badminton academy for kids in Regina. "You mean like the backyard thing?" That question came up more often than we would like to admit. Not anymore.

Over the past two registration cycles, our National Smash badminton program has been the first to fill every single block. The Smash Foundation tier — aimed at kids 6 to 10 — had a waitlist within the first five days of sign-up. And it was not because we ran some flashy marketing campaign. Word spread the old-fashioned way: parents talking to other parents at school pick-up.

## What Changed?

Badminton is not what most people picture. The shuttle can travel over 400 km/h in professional play, making it the fastest racket sport on the planet. But what draws parents in here in Saskatchewan has less to do with speed and more to do with accessibility. A child who has never held a racket can feel the satisfaction of a clean rally within their very first session. Compare that to basketball or soccer, where it can take weeks before a new player touches the ball during an actual game. Badminton gives every kid a turn, every single rally.

## The Coaching Difference

Most schools in the Regina Public and Catholic systems include a two-week badminton unit somewhere around Grade 6. That is about twenty minutes of instruction total, mixed in with regular PE. Our Smash program runs 16 sessions over 8 weeks with a strict 90-second instruction rule — meaning the coaches demonstrate quickly, then the kids hit. That ratio of instruction to movement is something we borrowed from high-performance training models used in Malaysia and Indonesia, where badminton is a cultural institution.

We use Yonex Mavis 300 and 350 nylon shuttles, not the cheap foam ones you find at a big-box store. The difference matters more than people think. A proper shuttle flies true and gives kids accurate feedback on their swing. When a six-year-old connects with a clean underhand serve and watches the shuttle arc perfectly over the net, you can see it click for them.

## A Sport That Travels With Them

One thing we tell parents is that badminton is a lifelong sport. You can play it at 8 or 80. There are community clubs across Canada, and the barriers to entry are remarkably low — you need a racket, a pair of indoor court shoes, and a net. Some of our families have started playing together on weekends at the Fieldhouse, and that kind of cross-generational participation is exactly what youth sport should be about.
