Rec league basketball is great. We will say that upfront. It gets kids moving, teaches basic teamwork, and gives families a reason to gather in a gymnasium on a Saturday morning. There is real value in that.

But rec league was never designed to develop individual skills. A typical rec session runs a few drills, splits kids into teams, and plays a scrimmage for the remaining 30 to 40 minutes. In a scrimmage, the ball naturally gravitates toward the two or three kids who are already confident. The others — the ones who need the most touches, the most encouragement, the most repetition — end up standing on the perimeter watching.

That is the gap our National Hoops program is built to fill.

## Structured Repetition Over Unstructured Scrimmage

Each of our 55-minute basketball sessions follows a progressive curriculum designed by our coaching staff. The sessions are broken into four blocks:

**Warm-Up and Footwork (8 minutes)** — This is not jogging around the gym. We run specific basketball footwork: jump stops, forward and reverse pivots, defensive slides, and close-out sprints. Footwork is the most overlooked fundamental in youth basketball and the hardest to retrofit later.

**Skill Block (20 minutes)** — This is the core of every session. One or two focused skills — for example, weak-hand dribbling or catch-and-shoot mechanics — drilled through progressive repetitions. We start with stationary work, add movement, then add a defender or decision point.

**Small-Sided Game Play (20 minutes)** — Instead of 5v5 full-court scrimmages, we play 3v3 and 4v4 on shortened courts. The math is simple: fewer players on the court means more touches per child, more decisions per minute, and more opportunities to apply the skill they just practised.

**Cool-Down and Coaching Debrief (7 minutes)** — Every session ends with a brief team huddle where coaches highlight what went well and set the focus for next session. This builds accountability and helps kids internalise the progression.

## Age-Appropriate Equipment

For our Junior Hoops tier (ages 6 to 10), we lower the rims to 8.5 or 9 feet and use size 5 or 6 basketballs. This is a detail that makes a massive difference. A ten-year-old shooting at a regulation 10-foot rim with a men's size 7 ball will develop a heaving, chest-push shot just to reach the basket. That is a bad habit that takes years to un-learn. By using appropriate equipment, kids build proper shooting form — balance, arc, follow-through — from the beginning.

## Coach-to-Athlete Ratios

Our Junior Hoops tier runs at a 1:10 to 1:12 ratio. Youth Hoops (ages 11 to 15) runs at 1:12 to 1:14. That means every child receives direct coaching feedback multiple times per session. In a rec league with one volunteer parent coaching 18 kids, that kind of individual attention simply is not possible.
