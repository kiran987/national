A parent came to us last spring with a question we hear more and more often: "My daughter is 8. She loves basketball, but her travel team coach says she needs to focus on basketball year-round if she wants to be competitive. Is that true?"

Our answer was direct: at 8 years old, no. And the research is overwhelming on this point.

## What Early Specialisation Actually Does

The Canadian Sport for Life framework — which informs Sport Canada's Long-Term Athlete Development (LTAD) model — explicitly recommends that children under 12 participate in multiple sports rather than specialising in one. The reasoning is both physiological and psychological.

Physically, different sports develop different movement patterns. Basketball builds vertical explosiveness, lateral agility, and hand-eye coordination through ball handling. Badminton develops wrist snap speed, split-step timing, and anteroposterior footwork. Volleyball trains platform control, spatial awareness, and cooperative timing. A child who plays all three is building a broader physical vocabulary than a child who plays only one, even if they play that one sport four days a week.

Psychologically, early single-sport specialisation is one of the strongest predictors of youth athlete burnout. A 2023 study published in the British Journal of Sports Medicine found that athletes who specialised in a single sport before age 12 were significantly more likely to quit competitive sport entirely by age 17. The dropout is not because they lost talent — it is because they lost interest.

## How NAA Is Structured for Multi-Sport Participation

We deliberately designed our programming so that families can enrol a child in more than one sport without schedule conflicts. Our fall block runs basketball and badminton on overlapping days, but volleyball sessions are scheduled separately. A child can do basketball and volleyball, or badminton and basketball, or all three.


## What About the Kid Who Only Wants Basketball?

That is completely fine. We are not saying every child must play three sports. If your child loves basketball and only wants to play basketball, they will get an excellent developmental experience in our National Hoops program.

What we are cautioning against is the pressure — usually from adults, not from children — to drop everything else in pursuit of one sport at an age when their bodies and brains are still developing fundamental movement patterns. The specialisation window will come. It does not need to arrive at age 8.

## The Real Competitive Advantage

Here is something that rarely gets mentioned in the "specialise early" conversation: many of the most successful athletes in Canadian university and professional sport were multi-sport participants through their early teens. They built diverse motor skills, avoided overuse injuries, and arrived at their primary sport with broader athletic intelligence.

The goal at NAA is not to produce professional athletes. It is to develop confident, physically literate young people who love sport and want to keep playing. If they happen to pursue high-performance competition later, the foundation we build will serve them well. If they become lifelong recreational players, that is a win too.
